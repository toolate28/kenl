# Safe Spiral Redstone Museum: Research & Learning Outcomes Framework
## Measuring Impact & Publishing Findings

---

## Core Research Questions

### Primary Question
**"Does interactive Redstone-based pedagogy improve understanding of AI/computational principles compared to traditional instruction?"**

### Secondary Questions
1. **Self-awareness:** Do children become better calibrated (understand their own uncertainty) after playing the Calibration Booth?
2. **Pattern recognition:** Do children demonstrate improved ability to identify emergent patterns after the Double Sixes exhibit?
3. **Strategy optimization:** Can children discover optimal decision strategies through gameplay (Reroller)?
4. **Systems thinking:** Do children develop intuitive understanding of information asymmetry and market mechanisms (Bid-Ask exhibit)?
5. **Scientific reasoning:** Do children demonstrate improved hypothesis-testing skills after the Light Bulb exhibit?

---

## Study Design Options

### Option A: Single-Group Intervention (Fastest, Weakest Evidence)
**Design:** Measure kids before and after all five exhibits
**Timeline:** 1-2 weeks of facilitation + analysis
**Sample:** 30-50 kids (one cohort)
**Outcome:** Pre/post score improvement
**Evidence Level:** Medium (no control group)
**Best For:** Proof of concept, early adoption

### Option B: Randomized Controlled Trial (Slower, Strongest Evidence)
**Design:** Random assignment to treatment (exhibits) or control (traditional lesson)
**Timeline:** 4-6 weeks of facilitation + analysis
**Sample:** 60-120 kids (30-60 treatment, 30-60 control)
**Outcome:** Treatment group vs control group improvement
**Evidence Level:** Very High (gold standard)
**Best For:** Academic publication, policy influence

### Option C: Longitudinal Follow-Up (Most Comprehensive)
**Design:** Follow kids for 8-12 weeks, measuring retention and transfer of learning
**Timeline:** 12 weeks
**Sample:** 40-60 kids
**Outcome:** Learning retention, transfer to other domains
**Evidence Level:** Very High
**Best For:** Understanding long-term impact

**Recommendation:** Start with Option A (4-6 weeks), then upgrade to Option B if resources allow.

## Measurement Instruments

### Pre/Post Test (All Options)

**Exhibit 1: Light Bulb - Scientific Method**
*Item 1:* You want to figure out which of three levers opens a door. You can test each lever once. Explain how you would design your test. (Open-ended, graded for hypothesis-test-observe-conclude structure)

*Item 2:* After testing, you think Lever A opens the door. How confident are you? Why? (Scale 1-10 + reasoning)

**Exhibit 2: Double Sixes - Pattern Recognition**
*Item 3:* Two random number generators produce values 1-6. On average, how many trials until both generators show a 6 simultaneously? Why? (Expected answer: ~36, reasoning about probability)

*Item 4:* If you ran the test 1,000 times, would the average be more stable, less stable, or the same as 100 times? Why? (Law of large numbers understanding)

**Exhibit 3: Calibration Booth - Self-Awareness**
*Item 5:* You make 10 predictions about random events. You claim 80% confidence on average. But you're only right 50% of the time. What does this tell you about yourself? (Metacognitive reasoning about overconfidence)

*Item 6:* Rate your confidence on each of the following statements, then we'll check your accuracy:
  - The capital of France is Paris (confidence: 1-10)
  - The speed of light is 300,000 km/s (confidence: 1-10)
  - Photosynthesis produces oxygen (confidence: 1-10)
  - The human brain has 100 billion neurons (confidence: 1-10)
  - Abraham Lincoln was born in 1800 (confidence: 1-10)
  
(Graded: accuracy of confidence relative to actual performance)

**Exhibit 4: The Reroller - Strategy Optimization**
*Item 7:* In a game where you roll a die and can choose to accept the value or roll again (but exceeding 21 loses the round), what threshold would you use to decide when to stop rolling? (Expected answer: 15-18, reasoning about probability)

*Item 8:* If you played this game 100 times with your strategy, vs 100 times with a "always stop at 10" strategy, which would score higher and why? (Comparative reasoning)

**Exhibit 5: Bid-Ask Spread - Systems Thinking**
*Item 9:* If buyers can't see seller prices and sellers can't see buyer prices, what is the "bid-ask spread" and why does it exist? (Understanding information asymmetry as root cause)

*Item 10:* What would happen to the bid-ask spread if both buyers and sellers could see each other's prices? Why? (Reasoning about information resolution)

### AI Literacy Assessment

**General AI Concepts** (all students, regardless of exhibit participation)

*Item 11:* What is a neural network and how does it learn? (Open-ended, graded for understanding of pattern-matching and iterative improvement)

*Item 12:* Why might an AI system be confident about something that's actually wrong? (Reasoning about hallucination, overconfidence, adversarial examples)

*Item 13:* What role does information play in AI decision-making? (Transfer of Redstone principles to AI context)

## Data Collection Protocol

### Timeline

**Week 0: Pre-Test**
- Administer all assessment items
- Record demographic info (age, prior Minecraft experience, AI familiarity)
- Establish baseline

**Weeks 1-2: Intervention**
- Facilitate exhibits
- Record gameplay decisions, reaction times, "aha moments"
- Detailed field notes on strategy evolution

**Week 2: Post-Test (Immediate)**
- Administer same assessment items
- Measure immediate learning

**Weeks 3-8: Optional Retention Measures**
- Re-test monthly
- Measure learning retention
- Measure transfer (applying principles to new contexts)

### Data Points to Collect

**Quantitative:**
- Pre/post test scores (percent correct)
- Confidence scores (self-assessed)
- Actual accuracy (measured)
- Gameplay decisions (which strategy chosen, when)
- Response times (how long to make decisions)
- Error rates (how often strategy fails)

**Qualitative:**
- Interview responses ("What did you learn?")
- Observation notes (frustration, joy, "aha moments")
- Student drawings/explanations
- Video recordings of gameplay + commentary
- Quotes capturing insight moments

### Sample Size Calculation

For detecting a medium effect size (Cohen's d = 0.5) with 80% power:
- **Single-group design:** n = 35-50
- **Two-group design (treatment vs control):** n = 60-80 total (30-40 per group)

## Analysis Plan

### Quantitative Analysis

**Primary Outcome:** Pre/post test score improvement

```
Treatment effect = Post-test score - Pre-test score
Percent improvement = (Post - Pre) / Pre × 100%
```

**Statistical test:** Paired t-test (if single group) or independent t-test (if comparing to control)

**Secondary analyses:**
- Subgroup analyses by age, prior Minecraft experience, AI familiarity
- Correlation between gameplay decisions and learning outcomes
- Strategy evolution over time (do kids optimize?)

### Qualitative Analysis

**Thematic coding:** Review observation notes and interviews for recurring themes
- "Aha moments" (when did insight occur?)
- Strategy changes (when did optimization begin?)
- Confusion points (where did kids struggle?)
- Transfer statements ("This is like..." indicating learning transfer)

**Video analysis:** Count instances of scientific reasoning behaviors (hypothesis-test-observe-conclude)

## Expected Results

### Hypothesis 1: Exhibit Effects
**Prediction:** Kids who play Exhibit 3 (Calibration) will show greater improvement on calibration items than kids who don't.
**Expected effect size:** d = 0.6-0.8
**Confidence:** High (principle directly addressed by exhibit)

### Hypothesis 2: Age Effects
**Prediction:** Older kids (14+) will show greater transfer to AI concepts than younger kids (8-12).
**Expected effect size:** d = 0.5
**Confidence:** Medium (abstract reasoning develops with age)

### Hypothesis 3: Prior Experience
**Prediction:** Kids with prior Minecraft experience will engage more deeply with circuits but show similar final learning.
**Expected effect size:** Engagement higher, learning similar
**Confidence:** Medium (Minecraft skill ≠ concept understanding)

### Hypothesis 4: Retention
**Prediction:** Learning will persist at 70%+ of immediate post-test level after 4 weeks.
**Expected effect size:** Retention ~80%
**Confidence:** Medium (depends on practice/reinforcement)

## Research Ethics

### Ethical Approval
- Obtain IRB (Institutional Review Board) approval for any formal study
- Get parental consent for kid participation
- Ensure video/photo release for any published materials
- Protect privacy (anonymize data, remove identifying info)

### Informed Consent
- Explain study to parents: "We're testing a new way to teach AI concepts"
- Explain to kids: "We want to see if you learn better through Minecraft circuits"
- Clarify: "This is voluntary, you can stop anytime, no grades involved"

### Data Protection
- Store data securely (encrypted, password-protected)
- Limit access to research team
- Delete data after analysis (or per ethics approval)
- De-identify before publication (remove names, ages if possible)

### Transparency
- Pre-register hypotheses (on OSF or similar) to prevent p-hacking
- Report both positive and negative results
- Acknowledge limitations honestly
- Share code/analysis for reproducibility

## Potential Limitations & How We'll Address Them

**Limitation:** Hawthorne effect (kids perform better because they're being studied)
**Address:** Include control group, long-term retention measures, real-classroom implementation

**Limitation:** Self-selection bias (kids who choose to play exhibit are already interested)
**Address:** Randomize exhibit selection, measure baseline interest, control for motivation

**Limitation:** Redstone is fun (confounding variable—did they learn or just enjoy?)
**Address:** Measure enjoyment separately, compare to traditional lesson + game, assess transfer

**Limitation:** Small sample size limits generalizability
**Address:** Start with proof of concept, scale to larger RCT, target diverse populations
